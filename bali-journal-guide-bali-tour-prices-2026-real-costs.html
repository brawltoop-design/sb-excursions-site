What Bali Tours and Excursions Actually Cost in 2026

Two couples can sit on the same boat to Nusa Penida having paid three times apart, and the one who overpaid never finds out. This is the honest 2026 price list for Bali excursions, transport and activities: what each one really costs, where the markup gets added, and how to spot a fair quote in five minutes.

What each Bali excursion should really cost

#1
Island day trip $60-85 pp

Nusa Penida day trip

The single most oversold day in Bali. Your fare covers a return fast boat from Sanur, a car and driver on the island, entrance fees at three or four viewpoints, and lunch. Hotel desks routinely quote $130 and up for exactly that. Anything under about $45 all-in cannot pay for the boat seat alone, so ask what got cut.

#2
Kintamani, 2am start $45-70 pp

Mount Batur sunrise trek

A joined trek with a licensed guide, hotel pickup around 2am, a headtorch and breakfast at the rim sits in the $45-70 band. Resort tour desks ask $90-140 for the same guide. The jeep version costs more because a jeep carries four. Pickup from Uluwatu or Canggu adds an hour each way, and honest operators charge for that openly.

#3
Nusa Penida boat $50-75 pp

Manta ray snorkelling

Three or four snorkel points off Penida, gear, a guide in the water and lunch. Fair range is $50-75 per person; touts on Sanur beach open at $120. Manta Point gets choppy and boats sometimes divert, which is a safety call rather than a scam. Check whether your operator offers a re-run or a refund when swell shuts the site.

#4
Lombok side, long day $95-150 pp

Gili Islands day trip

The boat is most of the cost. A return fast-boat seat from Padang Bai or Serangan runs $55-90 on its own, and land transfers plus port fees sit on top. Quotes near $250 are pure markup. Do the maths on time as well: you leave at dawn and get roughly five hours on the island before the last crossing.

#5
Per car, not per person $45-65 per car

Car with driver, full day

Eight to ten hours, fuel included, up to four or six passengers depending on the vehicle. Roughly 700,000 to 1,000,000 IDR is the going rate. Long routes to Amed, Lovina or West Bali cost more because of the distance. Entrance fees and parking are yours. If the price includes a coffee plantation stop, that stop is paying part of the driver's day.

#6
Ubud adventure $35-60 pp

Rafting and ATV rides

Ayung rafting with transfer, guide, gear and lunch lands around $35-55. A two-hour quad is $45-70 solo and less per head tandem. The number that moves is transfer distance, not the activity itself. Check the insurance line before you sign, and expect a photo package pitch at the end that is optional however firmly it is presented.

#7
Car-based route $45-70 per car

Waterfall and rice terrace day

This is a private driver day with a route attached, so price it as a car: $45-70 for the vehicle. Entrances run 20,000 to 50,000 IDR per waterfall and are almost never included. At Tukad Cepung and Tibumana you will be offered a compulsory-sounding local guide in the car park. It is not compulsory, and a tip is enough.

#8
DPS, per car $10-30 per car

Airport transfer

Seminyak or Kuta is 150,000-250,000 IDR. Canggu is 250,000-350,000. Ubud is 350,000-500,000 because it is over an hour each way. The official airport taxi counter charges roughly double, and the men calling out taxi in the arrivals corridor charge more again. Book ahead, get the driver's name and plate, and pay the agreed number.

#9
Charter, 15-30 min $180-450 pp

Helicopter scenic flight

Charged per helicopter, then split between seats. A 15-minute coastal loop works out around $180-280 each with three seats filled; 30 minutes over the volcanoes pushes toward $450. Flying as a couple costs far more per head. Weather cancellations are common in the wet season, so ask what happens to your money when a flight is scrubbed.

One boat, three prices

Take a joined day trip to Nusa Penida. The operator who owns the seat on the boat has a net rate, and that rate barely moves all year. What moves is how many people stand between that operator and you, because each one prices off the number they were handed, not off the original cost.

The chain usually runs like this. A wholesaler books boat seats in blocks and resells them to agents. An agent sells to a hotel tour desk. The tour desk quotes you. A resort concierge in Seminyak or Nusa Dua typically works on 20-40%, and at the top end of the market it can reach half. That desk is not running your tour. It is forwarding your name and your room number.

Street sellers arrive at the same place by a different road. The laminated boards outside minimarts in Kuta and Legian, and the men who approach you on the sand, buy at a net rate and then read you. Your hotel, your shoes and how long you have been in the country all set the opening number. That is why the quote drops sharply the moment you start walking away: the first figure was never a price, it was a test.

Add an international booking platform on top, with its own 20-30% cut and surge pricing in July and August, and you can see how one seat on one boat reaches one traveller at $60 and another at $180. Where the money actually goes on a Penida day

It helps to see the inside of one price. A joined Nusa Penida day at the fair end of the range is carrying, per person: a return fast boat seat, which is the largest single line and costs roughly $22-38 on its own; a share of an island car and driver for six or seven hours; entrance and parking tickets at every viewpoint, which run 25,000 to 50,000 IDR a stop and add up to 100,000-200,000 IDR across a normal route; the driver-guide's day; lunch; port and insurance fees; and the operator's margin.

Do that arithmetic and a $35 all-in Penida day stops looking like a bargain. The boat alone eats most of it. Something has to give, and it is usually one of four things: an overloaded boat, no passenger insurance, a schedule so tight that Kelingking becomes twenty minutes in a queue, or a day that quietly acquires two shopping stops where the operator earns commission on you instead of from you.

Penida is also a genuinely hard island to run tours on. The roads down to Kelingking and Diamond Beach are narrow and steep, the ferry timetable bunches everyone into the same two-hour window, and crossings get cancelled outright in bad swell. Absorbing all of that is part of what a real operator is charging for. Our full-day Nusa Penida tour is priced to include the boat and the fees rather than to look cheap and collect at the gate.

Red flags: when the price itself is the scam

Most overcharging in Bali is not a con with a fake tour at the end. You get a real boat and a real guide. You simply pay two or three times what the person beside you paid. These are the signals that you have been put in the expensive lane.

  • They ask where you are staying before quoting. Your hotel is the price. A fixed rate does not need your room number.
  • The number drops sharply when you walk away. A 40% discount in ten seconds means the opening figure was invented.
  • Cash, in full, in advance, on the street. A real operator takes a deposit and sends a written confirmation with a company name on it.
  • Nobody will name the operator. Ask which company runs the boat and which harbour it leaves from. A seller who cannot answer is three links down a chain.
  • “Free hotel pickup” from a stranger on the beach. The pickup is inside your price and always was. Honest operators name a pickup zone and charge openly for Uluwatu, Canggu or Ubud starts.
  • Entrance fees appear at the gate. Get the all-in figure in writing, including viewpoint tickets, parking, gear and lunch.
  • “Almost full, decide now.” Manufactured urgency on a beach is not a booking system.
  • The price is quoted only in US dollars, cash. The implied exchange rate is usually poor. Ask for the rupiah figure and compare.

None of this is unique to tours. The same pressure shows up around money changers, scooter damage claims and taxi meters, which we cover in the Bali scams and safety guide.

A five-minute price check before you book

You do not need to haggle. You need to compare like with like, and that takes about five minutes.

Ask who operates the tour, not who is selling it: a company name, the boat or vehicle, and a phone number that answers. Then ask for one all-in figure in writing covering entrance tickets, parking, lunch, equipment and insurance, so nothing surfaces at a gate later. Ask where the pickup zone is and what a pickup from your area costs, because a Sanur departure with an Ubud pickup means somebody drives ninety minutes each way before your day even starts. Ask the group size too, since “small group” covers everything from eight people to twenty-two on one boat.

Then compare three quotes on the same inclusions rather than three headline numbers. Pay a deposit and settle the balance on the day; that is normal practice here and it protects both sides. Where you can, pay in rupiah by transfer or QRIS.

Two practical notes. Prices rise 10-20% in July, August and over Christmas and New Year, so an off-peak quote is not comparable to a peak one. And for everyday movement rather than excursions, a metered Bluebird or a ride-hailing app usually beats any tour desk, as the transport guide explains. For a full flexible day, a car with driver at a fixed rate removes the negotiation entirely.

Bali tour price questions, answered straight

How much does a day tour in Bali cost in 2026?

A joined group day tour with transport, a guide and lunch generally sits between $45 and $85 per person. Boat-based days like Nusa Penida or the Gilis run higher because the crossing is the main cost. A private car with a driver is $45-65 for the whole vehicle rather than per head, which is usually cheapest for three or four people.

Why is the same tour cheaper online than at my hotel?

Because the hotel desk is a reseller. Concierges and tour desks typically add 20-40%, and at large resorts it can reach half. They are not operating the tour, they are forwarding your booking. Going direct to the operator, or to an agency that publishes its rate, removes that layer without changing the boat, guide or vehicle you get.

Is it cheaper to book Bali tours after you arrive?

Sometimes, but not reliably, and not from the beach. Local walk-in agencies can undercut international platforms because they skip the platform fee. Street sellers do the opposite and price off how you look. In July, August and around New Year, boats and jeeps sell out, so arriving with nothing booked means paying whatever is left.

How much should a private driver cost for a day in Bali?

Roughly 700,000 to 1,000,000 IDR, about $45-65, for eight to ten hours with fuel included and four to six passengers depending on the vehicle. Longer runs to Amed, Lovina or West Bali cost more. Entrance fees, parking and the driver's lunch are extra by custom, and a tip of 50,000-100,000 IDR is normal after a good day.

Are entrance fees included in Bali tour prices?

Often not, and that is where surprise costs appear. Waterfalls charge 20,000-50,000 IDR each, Nusa Penida viewpoints 25,000-50,000 a stop, and temples add a sarong rental. Ask for one all-in figure in writing before you pay anything. When a quote looks unusually low, missing entrance tickets is the most common reason.

Is a very cheap Nusa Penida tour safe?

Treat anything under about $45 all-in with suspicion. A return fast boat seat alone costs $22-38, so the rest has to come from somewhere: an overloaded boat, no passenger insurance, a rushed schedule, or shopping stops. Cheap is a different risk from expensive. Ask about the boat operator, the passenger count and what happens if the crossing is cancelled.

How much is a Bali airport transfer supposed to be?

From Ngurah Rai, 150,000-250,000 IDR to Kuta or Seminyak, 250,000-350,000 to Canggu, and 350,000-500,000 to Ubud. The official taxi counter charges around double that, and drivers touting inside the terminal charge more again. Booking a fixed-price transfer in advance is the simplest way to skip the first negotiation of your trip.

Should I pay for tours in cash or by card?

Pay a deposit by card or bank transfer and settle the balance in rupiah on the day. That gives you a paper trail and keeps some leverage if the trip is not as described. Avoid handing over the full amount in US dollar cash on a street: the implied exchange rate is usually poor and you have no recourse.
