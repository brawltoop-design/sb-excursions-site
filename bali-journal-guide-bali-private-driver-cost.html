Private Driver in Bali: Cost Per Day and How It Works

A private driver in Bali costs from $59 per car per day, and the price covers the whole vehicle — a couple pays the same as a small group sharing the car. For that you get a car with its own driver for a full flexible day, typically 10-12 hours, with hotel pickup on your schedule and a route you can change as you go. This guide breaks down what the day rate covers, when a driver beats Grab and metered taxis, the six ways travelers actually use one — from waterfall loops to an airport transfer from $15 — and how booking works over WhatsApp with no prepayment. Everything here comes from the routes we drive every week, not from aggregator listings.

What from $59 per car covers, and who fits

A private car with driver in Bali costs from $59 per car per day — one figure for the whole vehicle, not a per-person rate. The car takes a small group, so a couple and a family end up paying the same, which is why the per-head cost drops fast the more of you share the ride. The booking is flexible daily use: pickup is from your hotel on your schedule, and the service covers all major Bali regions, so nothing stops you from starting in Uluwatu and ending the day in Ubud.

The honest fine print: a per-car day rate depends on the route. A loop around one area and a full crossing of the island are different days for the driver, so send your stop list on WhatsApp and you get one complete quote before you commit — with no prepayment at any point. Entrance tickets at temples and waterfalls are small and paid at each gate, so keep some cash on hand for those.

Private driver vs Grab, Gojek and taxis

For a single short hop inside one area, ride-hailing wins — a cross-town Grab or Gojek ride costs a few dollars and arrives in minutes. The math flips the moment your day has multiple stops: at waterfalls, temples and viewpoints there is often no driver nearby for the return leg, some villages restrict ride-hailing pickups entirely, and mobile signal at the prettiest places is exactly as unreliable as you would expect. A private driver removes the whole problem — the car waits at every stop, and the day has one price agreed in advance instead of six separate fares.

Metered taxis cover the airport and the southern resort strip but are not a realistic touring tool — waiting time and long inland returns get negotiated on the spot, which rarely ends in your favor. For the full picture of scooters, shuttles and fast boats, see our getting around Bali guide.

How a driver day actually runs

A typical full driver day runs 10-12 hours door to door, and the route stays yours the whole way. Most guests send a rough list the evening before — three to five stops makes a comfortable day — and the driver sequences them around traffic and opening hours. Distances are the real constraint: the southern hotel areas sit about 45-90 minutes from Ubud depending on traffic, and the far east or the north coast runs roughly 2-3 hours each way, so one far region per day is the sensible ceiling.

The difference from a fixed tour is control. Our Ubud highlights tour from $69 runs a proven 10-hour route that already works; a driver day starts from a blank map. Take the fixed tour when you want the greatest hits handled for you, and the driver day when you have your own list — a specific warung, a beach club at sunset, or a slow morning that fixed departures do not allow.

How to book a driver on WhatsApp

Booking takes one message: your dates, hotel area, group size and a rough list of stops. You get back a complete day quote and a confirmation in writing — no prepayment, no deposit, and cancelling is a message rather than a form. What to put in that first message:

  • Dates and hotel area — the quote depends on where the day starts
  • Group size and luggage — so the right car is assigned
  • Your stop list, even a rough one — the driver sequences it around traffic and opening hours
  • Early starts or late finishes — sunrise pickups are fine; the exact pickup time is confirmed on WhatsApp the day before

If you are still shaping the trip, start from the scenario cards above — a waterfall loop, a temple circuit or a photo day each fill a driver day well — and we will tell you honestly on WhatsApp if a fixed-route tour covers your list for less. The same chat stays open during the day itself, so route changes on the move take one message, not a renegotiation.

FAQ

How much does a private driver cost in Bali per day?

A private driver in Bali costs from $59 per car per day booked direct with SB Excursions over WhatsApp. That figure is for the whole vehicle, not per seat, and covers a full flexible day of driving — typically 10-12 hours — with hotel pickup on your schedule.

Is a Bali private driver priced per car or per person?

Per car. The from $59 per car rate covers the whole vehicle for the day, so two people and a small group sharing the car pay the same figure. That is the main reason a private driver undercuts per-person tours once three or more travelers share the ride.

Does the daily rate include fuel and parking?

Confirm this on WhatsApp before you book — send your exact route and you get one complete day quote, so there are no surprises at parking barriers or fuel stops. Route length changes what a fair day rate looks like, which is why every quote is per itinerary.

Can I change the route during the day?

Yes. Route flexibility is the point of hiring a driver instead of joining a fixed tour — you can extend a stop you love, skip one that looks crowded, or add a restaurant on the way back. Big direction changes are easiest if you mention them at pickup.

Do Bali private drivers speak English?

Confirm on WhatsApp when you book. Tell us if an English-speaking driver matters for your day — for a route where you want commentary and recommendations, say so up front; for a pure driving day, navigation apps and simple English cover almost everything travelers need.

How many hours do you get a driver for?

The booking is flexible daily use rather than a strict hour count, and a typical full day runs 10-12 hours door to door. Shorter days are fine — an afternoon of two or three stops — but the flat day rate makes long multi-stop routes the best value.

Is a private driver cheaper than Grab in Bali?

Not for one short hop — a cross-town Grab ride costs a few dollars and wins easily. For a day with several stops, the private car wins: no re-booking at remote spots with weak signal, no surge pricing, and the car waits while you explore each place.

Do I need to pay a deposit to book a driver in Bali?

No. There is no prepayment — you book over WhatsApp, get the day confirmed in writing, and settle at the end of the day. The exact pickup time is confirmed on WhatsApp the day before, so sunrise starts and late finishes are easy to arrange.
