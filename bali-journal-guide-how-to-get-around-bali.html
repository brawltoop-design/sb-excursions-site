How to Get Around Bali: 7 Transport Options Compared

The best way to get around Bali is a combination: a private car with driver for sightseeing days, ride-hailing apps for short hops inside town, and fast boats for the islands — because Bali has no trains and no useful public buses. A flexible driver day starts from $59 per car, a pre-booked airport transfer from $15, and fast boats to Nusa Penida or the Gilis from $15. This guide compares all seven realistic options, including the two most people get wrong: where Grab and Gojek actually work, and why we tell most visitors to skip the scooter unless they already ride at home. Decide your transport mix before you land and the whole island gets easier — most of Bali's daily friction is people improvising rides on the curb.

Why Bali has no real public transport

Bali has no train network, no metro, and no public bus system that reaches the places visitors actually go. A handful of government bus routes circle Denpasar, but they miss the beaches, the waterfalls and the temple circuits, and they are not built around hotel areas or luggage. The practical result is that everyone — locals included — moves on private wheels: cars with drivers, ride-hailing apps, metered taxis and scooters. Distances mislead here. On the map, Canggu to Uluwatu looks like a short hop; on the road it is about 60-90 minutes depending on traffic, because nearly every route funnels through the same two-lane corridors. Plan transport the way you plan hotels: decide the mode for each day before you land, not on the curb outside your villa.

The mix that works for most visitors is simple: a private car with driver for full sightseeing days, from $59 per car; Grab or Gojek for short hops inside town; and fast boats for anything across the water. If you are tempted by a scooter, read our safety, scams and health guide first — it covers the license, insurance and police-checkpoint reality that rental shops will not mention. And if you are still sketching the trip itself, our 7-day itinerary shows how driving days, beach days and island days fit together.

When Grab and Gojek work — and when they do not

Grab and Gojek work well inside the main tourist towns — Kuta, Seminyak, Canggu, Sanur, Ubud and Denpasar — and poorly almost everywhere else. Inside those zones the apps are cheap, the price is fixed on screen before you ride, and a car usually arrives within minutes; the motorbike option is the fastest way through southern traffic if you travel alone without luggage. The apps break down in three situations. Remote areas: east and north Bali have few or no drivers, so you can ride out but not back. Community-restricted zones: some villages and beach areas limit app pickups in favor of local drivers, so the app accepts your booking and then no car comes. Long one-way routes: a driver facing a two-hour empty return will often cancel late, which can strand you at a waterfall an hour before dark.

The airport deserves its own note. The apps do operate there, but pickups happen at designated zones away from the arrivals door, and finding them with luggage after a red-eye is nobody's favorite start to a holiday. Most people find a pre-booked airport transfer from $15 calmer: the driver tracks the flight, waits with a name sign, and the exact pickup time is confirmed on WhatsApp the day before. Use the same logic in reverse for departure day, when a cancelled app ride costs far more than it saves.

A driver for the day vs three or four Grab rides

For any day with more than two stops, a private driver beats chaining Grab rides — and the reason is not fare math, it is failure points. Every app leg on a sightseeing route is a separate gamble: coverage at the pickup point, a driver willing to take the route, a late cancellation, a wait in the heat with your bags. Waterfall and temple stops make it worse, because you are booking the return ride from exactly the kind of remote spot where the apps are thinnest. A driver removes all of it: the car waits at every stop, your things stay in the boot, the route reorders itself around rain or traffic, and there is one price for the whole day instead of four separate negotiations with an algorithm.

  • Private car with driver — from $59 per car: flexible daily use, hotel pickup on your schedule, priced per vehicle so a group of four splits one fare
  • Ubud rice terrace, temple and volcano day — from $69: a ready-made 10-hour route if you would rather not plan stops yourself
  • Grab or Gojek: keep them for what they are good at — short in-town hops, food runs, and the ride home from dinner

The per-car pricing is the detail most people miss: a driver day is a fixed cost shared across the group, while app rides multiply per trip and per detour. For the full breakdown — what moves the price, how routes are agreed, how tipping works — see our private driver cost guide.

Reaching the islands: how fast boats actually work

Fast boats are the only practical way to reach Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan and the Gili Islands — there are no bridges and no scheduled flights on these routes. Boats to Penida and Lembongan leave mainly from Sanur harbor, with a crossing of roughly 30-45 minutes depending on sea conditions. Boats to the Gilis and Lombok run from Padang Bai on the east coast and take about 90 minutes to 2.5 hours depending on the boat and its stops. Two things surprise first-timers: boarding is often a wet-feet walk straight off the sand, and each harbor collects a small fee in cash on top of the ticket. Morning departures matter more than the brand on the hull — seas are calmer before midday, especially in the windier months.

Book the crossing and the road transfer as one plan, not two. A fast boat transfer starts from $15, and the harbor-side scrum — porters, resellers, three boats boarding at once — is far easier when someone has already told you on WhatsApp which counter and which boat is yours. The drive to the harbor is its own leg: depending on where you stay, Sanur or Padang Bai sits anywhere from 15 to 90 minutes away in traffic, so pad the departure time. FAQ

Is Grab available in Bali?

Yes. Grab and Gojek both operate in Bali and work well inside the main towns — Kuta, Seminyak, Canggu, Sanur, Ubud and Denpasar. Coverage fades fast in east and north Bali, some villages restrict app pickups, and drivers often cancel long one-way routes, so do not rely on the apps outside town.

Can you get around Bali without a scooter?

Yes, easily. Most visitors never ride one: private drivers cover sightseeing days from $59 per car, Grab and Gojek handle short hops in town, and fast boats reach the islands. A scooter only makes sense if you hold a motorcycle license and already ride confidently at home.

How do I get from Bali airport to my hotel?

Three options: a pre-booked transfer from $15 with a driver waiting at arrivals, the official airport taxi counter with fixed zone rates, or Grab from its designated pickup points. After a night flight the pre-booked car is the calmest — no negotiation, and the driver tracks your flight if it lands late.

Is there Uber in Bali?

No. Uber left Southeast Asia in 2018 and sold its operations to Grab. In Bali the two ride-hailing apps are Grab and the Indonesian app Gojek — both work the way Uber does, with the price fixed in the app before you ride. Download both; coverage differs street by street.

How much does a private driver cost in Bali?

A private car with driver starts from $59 per car for flexible daily use, and the price is per vehicle, not per person, so a group of four splits one fare. You agree the route in advance and pay once for the whole day; entry tickets and lunch stops stay on you.

Are taxis in Bali metered and safe?

Bluebird taxis are — they run real meters and can be booked through the MyBluebird app, which avoids the lookalike cabs painted a similar shade of blue. Outside Bluebird, street taxis in tourist areas often quote flat invented fares. If a driver refuses the meter, get out and book through the app.

How do you get to Nusa Penida or the Gili Islands from Bali?

By fast boat, from $15. Boats to Nusa Penida leave mainly from Sanur harbor and cross in roughly 30-45 minutes depending on the sea; boats to the Gilis run from Padang Bai and take about 90 minutes to 2.5 hours. Book morning departures — seas are calmer before midday.

Do I need a license to rent a scooter in Bali?

Yes. Legally you need a motorcycle license from home plus an international driving permit, and police checkpoints in tourist areas do stop foreigners. Without the license your travel insurance is usually void the moment you ride, which is a bigger risk than any fine. Rental shops will not warn you.
